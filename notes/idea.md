## The sentence

> On this page, **the ticked line items of an 1851 account from Main and Olive Street,
> St. Louis — and the aged claim that is its direct descendant** — become **the fixed half
> of the split stage: one claim stays on screen for the whole visit, being worked line by
> line, while the argument scrolls past it.**

### What the archive gave us
The found artefact is `assets/account-of-messrs-bleifuss-with-wilson-brothers.jpg`: an
itemised account, "St. Louis, Mo. April 30 1851", bought of Wilson & Brothers, Corner of
Main & Olive Street. Handwritten line items, amounts in two ruled columns, a running total
carried "Forward" — and **a tick in the left margin beside every line a person checked**.

Revyve's mark is a check. The tick in the margin of a St. Louis account book and the tick
in Revyve's logo are the same gesture: a person confirming a line. That is the through-line
of the page — the work has not changed in 175 years, only who does the repetitive part of
it. It is why "nothing goes out without a person on anything that matters" is the promise,
and why the page can make it without a word of explanation.

### The competitor test
Put "another medical billing company" in the sentence. A competitor can show a claim —
but it cannot show *this* device: a claim that keeps ageing as you read, crosses the
120-day line, and logs every action with the evidence behind it. Those are Revyve's own
published promises, not the category's:

- "Anything over 120 days gets a status check, a reason and a next action." (/for-practices/)
- "Each claim is tied to its encounter, its payer evidence, the person responsible for it
  and the next step allowed. Nothing lives only in someone's inbox." (/revyve-ai/)
- "Every action is logged with its evidence and its outcome." (/revyve-ai/)

Competitors sell software. Revyve owns the work — so the work, not a product shot, is
what stays on screen.

### Why the palette belongs to the idea
Green-bar fanfold ledger paper — pale green on bone — is the actual material of a billing
office. `green-black-bone` is that stock inverted. The colour is not decoration; it is
the subject's own paper.

## The one live thing

**The claim selector.** Three real-shaped aged claims. Picking one drives the whole page:

- the fixed half shows that claim's record (`data-ui`), ageing against the 120-day line
- the showcase section shows that same claim's raw payer response on the dark half and
  the tidy prepared record on the light half

One state, one control, two expressions — not a playground.

Rules it must meet: real content; works with a thumb on a phone; a plain fallback that
renders all three claims as an ordinary list with no script; never blocks reading.

## How the signature move carries the idea
"Slow crossfade" = the fixed half quietly changes as its matching section arrives. For us
the claim advances through its real lifecycle as the reader scrolls:
submitted → denied → ageing → status check → evidence attached → biller review → resolved.
The ritual of the work, performed by the scroll.

## Guard against this direction's known failure (sheet 11)
"The commonest complaint was monotony: several screens of the same split."
- how-it-works breaks it with the printed slip (a table, not a split)
- showcase breaks it with a full-width dark/light field pair
- cta is an almost empty single-column screen
- faq is a two-column reading grid
- footer is full-bleed photograph
Only the hero holds the true 50/50 split.
